# LaTeX

## Descriptions

- Offered by: Compiled from the official LaTeX project and Chinese material from the CTEX community ([LaTeX project](https://www.latex-project.org/))

LaTeX is a typesetting system built on TeX. Knuth developed TeX and Lamport wrapped LaTeX around it, both towering figures in computing. But famous authors are not a reason to learn it. What separates it from WYSIWYG software like Word is the division of labor: you write the content and the system handles typesetting. Heading levels, figure numbering, formula spacing, and bibliography formatting are all governed by style rules, so someone with no typesetting experience can still produce professionally laid out documents.

That trait shows most clearly in long documents. By the hundredth page, manual formatting costs rise exponentially, whereas LaTeX keeps every new section consistent with the style defined at the start, with numbering and tables of contents updating automatically. Mathematics is its traditional strength, and complex fractions, matrices, and multiple subscripts are naturally easier to write than in any GUI.

The path is fairly clear, and the hard part is at the start. Environment setup is the first blocker, since a local TeX distribution often lacks packages and fonts; an online editor such as [Overleaf](https://www.overleaf.com/) skips this step and ships plenty of ready templates.
